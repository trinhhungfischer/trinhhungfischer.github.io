# Round Up

![Header image](/images/about-me-header.png)

Perhaps this is the longest article and took the most time to write by me, a Game Designer who has been trying to work in the industry for the past 3 years. It's not long enough but definitely not a short period either. There is really very little time to sit down and summarize my working journey over the past 3 years, from the stage of being a stumbling apprentice with almost no knowledge, treating what senior Nhat said as a bible, to someone who has experienced more, gradually trying to find good answers and a solid foothold, understanding a little bit more.

Maybe through this article, I want to summarize a bit about my working process, a few projects, and my future orientation to continue developing in my career. Honestly, I really want to maximize my abilities in a job that I still have enough passion for and love more than anything else.

# What I've Done in the Past 3 Years

> I make Games, I learn how to make Games, I learn how to work with people, and I learn to play Games.

That short sentence is probably simple enough to describe what I've done a lot, constantly throughout the entire 3 years.

## The Very Beginning

I often tell the story of the coincidental event of why I became a Game Designer through a decision made by dice. Well, people might think it's a joke or just for fun, but it's the truth haha. If anyone finds it too long, you can skip to "The Very Beginning 2".

> 🗣 **A quite long narrative story here:**
> 
> After my first Game Jam acting as a GD, I was really interested and wanted to learn more about this seemingly exciting job, especially after being laid off from a Game Dev position at company V.
> 
> I was quite intrigued because I thought at that time, being at the end of my junior year of university, it wasn't too early or too late to try something new. I got an opportunity to be mentored by Mr. Hung Vu. I cherished that opportunity, but I was very hesitant about the first meeting to start the story haha. At that time, I really didn't know what to choose next. And I had 6 D6 dice and 3 choices, and I assigned point ranges for the 3 choices and made one fateful roll.
> 
> Here are the three choices and the point milestones I assigned to them:
> 1. Go meet Mr. Hung Vu and seriously pursue this new job step-by-step (1 - 20)
> 2. Find a Unity Dev internship (21 - 30)
> 3. Go back to my advising professor's company and continue doing Data Science (31 - 36)
> 
> And the result of that dice roll was 18, and **I am a Game Designer**!
> 
> Haha, do you realize the absurdity in the story above? I only realized it later. It's that rolling 6D6 cannot start from the value of 1, but from 6-36. At that time, I truly thought the probability for choice 1 was **20/36**. People might laugh at a math major, but it's some sort of effect where the things you are most subjective about are often the most prone to errors kk, luckily the ratio of option 1 was still nearly 50% I think haha.
> 
> **P/S:**
> - First, people might ask if the choice above was interesting. According to game theory, I'm having a very fun game because I got a gacha result, a very real-life gacha game. I think no matter which choice I fell into, I would still be very happy with it, and choice one is still very fun now and will be for a few more years.
> - Second, perhaps in an alternate timeline, the dice landed on a different face. I strongly believe in the multiverse theory haha, so if anyone asks, try building a multiverse travel machine and ask.

## The Very Beginning 2

I used my salary and Game Jam prize money to buy my first book about Games and making Games to read. I was very impressed by this job, very excited about it even though it was just the beginning when I got to work and be mentored a lot by Mr. Hung Vu. Perhaps he didn't provide too much hard knowledge but left me with many perspectives on the job and the industry.

Maybe that was the most immature, most starter period of 3 or 4 months, where I hadn't accurately defined what I needed to do, what I needed to make games in a larger team,... Probably I wasn't passionate enough to dig deeper, maybe so, but at that time I still had school work to complete.

## The First Official Position

Honestly, after those initial steps, I really wanted to find an internship, a place to apprentice... it sounds a bit sad but it seemed like not many people wanted a GD intern at that time. Nowadays they hire a lot for level designer interns too. But back then, I went to ABI for an interview and talked to a senior with the same name. Hearing about my work experience, he suggested I return to a data role. Well, I think I refused, it seems I was quite determined at that time.

After that, I genuinely got an interview for a GD position at Zitga in the first days of 2023. My current company and my current team. There really wasn't much to say about the interview; basically, I was like a deer caught in the headlights with new knowledge. I'm truly grateful to Z for giving a clueless kid their first GD apprenticeship position.

## The Early Days of Being a GD

Back then, my apprenticeship contract allowed me to study one session at school and work one session a week at the company. Honestly, joining a new company and a team larger than my previous one made me feel a bit overwhelmed. My contact time with people was very little in the first two months or so. My very initial tasks were probably trying to talk and start conversations with more people, playing a game, trying to understand the processes, reading company documents... People might think it has very little to do with professional skills, right? I admit it has very little to do with professional skills, but it was more meaningful for a quiet and very new person like me.

## The First GD Class

By a lucky coincidence, almost right after I joined Zitga, a special GD also joined the company shortly after, Mr. Nhat. It was a course that didn't just give me many perspectives on the game industry, the job, games, and how to look at passion; perhaps those are things that are inherently basic for every GD. But for me, it was like a bible. Just look at how I took notes on paper and synthesized them on Notion to understand how much I idolized him. I synthesized, found images, and rewrote things according to my understanding.

## The Very First Project

For a very, very early project, perhaps my biggest regret about this unsuccessful project was my ability to work with people and my ability to stop and speak up when things weren't right.

Even though I was very, very sad because I put so much effort into everything, in terms of design, level design, character design... Partly it might be due to my personality of respecting everyone; when I was the main GD for the project, I couldn't speak up about many things. It seems there was a lot of immaturity in expressing thoughts. A project that taught me many things about how to work with devs and with people at the very first steps.

## The First "Okay" Project

I wasn't the main GD for this project; I only supported the Mechanics Docs and Balancing. At that time, I was still trying to save and do everything for the "very first project" during its decline. It was really okay, very good, very nice, and truly made me happy again in the late days of 2023. I'll probably stop telling about the project here; talking at length would violate the NDA haha.

## I Learned to Play Games

It seems I talked a lot about projects, but honestly, what really hooked me during those months was Games and the work. Perhaps before making games, playing games was just a recreational hobby, but maybe this period was when I became addicted to games. I started learning to play LOL and I guess I got addicted to it, making my graduation project look more like an unfinished product. The time doing my graduation project overlapped with the timeline of the project right after the "okay" project, meaning late 2023 to early 24, which was also when I was most addicted to LOL. Well, I never thought I could get that addicted haha. On weekends, I could play almost all day for both days and hardly did anything extra for my project. That's why I didn't let anyone see my project because, to me, it inherently wasn't complete compared to my initial design, so it was never finished.

After that, I thought I should rehab and play more genres, watch more genres, know more things, and love games more. And that's just Digital games; I also really, really love Board Games. I love them so much, I can play from 8 AM to 10 PM. The little things and their mechanics are truly fascinating.

# What Will I Do in the Next 3 Years?

## I Want to Achieve a Milestone

Before leaving Zitga, I really want to have something very impressive, very new. It might not be a million-dollar successful game, but achieving it would definitely be a milestone. But for me, a milestone would be a very special advancement in my professional skills, like mastering a certain power, publishing a certain article, having a game-changing insight, like an academic paper. Recently, I feel I am very close to that milestone, or at least I see signs of achieving clearer steps towards that milestone than before.

## I Will Keep Stepping, Keep Swimming

I don't want to stay fixed at Z; perhaps new challenges will continuously come during my working process. But I feel an opportunity to change environments, an opportunity to meet people who make games in a very different environment, for example, abroad. Or in an Indie environment making PC games.

So maybe I will return to Z or work with Z in some way, but I will definitely keep swimming before I turn 30.
